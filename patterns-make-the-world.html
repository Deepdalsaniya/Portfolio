Quantitative Research Instrument · Research-only

Built to prove or kill its own edge, without lying to itself.

A daily instrument that scans every liquid US stock for chart-reversal patterns, ranks them by decades of walk-forward backtesting, and refuses to claim an edge it hasn’t earned. What follows is less about the patterns than about the judgment behind them: the times the honest move was to demote my own signal, distrust my own data, or report a number getting worse.

I build market-research tools, and the temptation in that work is always the same: to make the backtest look good. This project is my answer to that temptation. It’s a working instrument, a universe of roughly 1,600 US stocks over $4B, scanned every day for double-bottoms, double-tops, and head-and-shoulders formations, but its real design goal was to be impossible to fool, including by me.

01 · ARCHITECTURE

Three reads, kept honest and separate.

It makes three independent reads on each name and never lets them fake agreement.

i.

Pattern

The price geometry: where the base, neckline, and target actually sit.

ii.

Options

A positioning estimate that cannot tell buy from sell, so it’s labeled that way and never gates a call.

iii.

Causal chain

A fundamental thesis I rule on by hand; shown as context, never allowed to block an idea.

02 · THE JUDGMENT, NOT THE PATTERNS

Six times the honest call cost me something.

Killing darlings

I demoted my own signals when the data disagreed.

The backtest was blunt. The bearish patterns lose in a market that drifts upward, so they’re flagged as caution and never as entries. And three signals I’d assumed mattered, RSI divergence, volume confirmation, and Wyckoff springs, showed no measurable edge across forty years. They stay on the board as context, but they’re kept out of the ranking score. The rule: if the walk-forward doesn’t pay for it, it doesn’t get to vote.

scored → trend-reclaim (measured edge)
demoted to context → RSI-divergence · volume-surge · springs (≈0 edge)
Survivorship

I caught a trap before it caught me.

The live outcome ledger showed 93 resolved trades, every one a loss, zero targets reached. The tempting read is “the edge is fake.” The correct read is that a target on a monthly setup takes over a year to resolve while a stop can fire in days, so at three days old the ledger can only contain fast failures. It’s left-censored, not evidence. I refused to fit anything to it, and said so out loud.

Honest metric

I fixed a number, watched it get worse, and shipped the worse one.

Someone noticed the neckline my detector drew sat above the actual candles. The cause: it was reading the intraday wick, not the candle body. I moved it to the body and the levels snapped to what a chart reader sees. Then I re-ran the calibration on a sample ten times larger, and the headline win-rate fell from 64% to 59%. The old number was small-sample optimism. The lower one is truer, so the lower one is what the board shows.

neckline: wick → body
KRC 45.03 → 42.89  ·  W-monthly odds 64% /n480 → 59% /n2310
Prior, not result

I labeled the expected-value model a hypothesis, not an edge.

I built an EV estimator on the backtest, and it produces a genuine fork rather than a tidy answer: the wider target maximizes value per trade, while the tighter one maximizes value per unit of time and by growth-optimal (Kelly) sizing. I exposed all three views and let the objective decide, instead of picking one to look decisive. And the whole table is stamped a hypothesis: its probabilities are backtest frequencies, not live-confirmed, and confirmation is months away. It’s a prior to be checked, never a claim.

Un-foolable by design

I built the ledger so it can’t peek at the future.

Every simulated decision is recorded append-only, with the date the data was known kept strictly separate from the date it resolved, so nothing can leak backward from the future. That discipline is what makes the backtest trustworthy, and it’s also what surfaced a bug: 848 simulated trades that could never have been taken, because a stop had been placed on the wrong side of the entry. I root-caused it, fixed it, and cleaned the forward record before it accumulated another day of noise.

leakage guard
data_as_of ≠ event_at · append-only · walk-forward
caught 848 un-takeable sims → root-caused & fixed
Fail loud

It breaks in the open, never in silence.

A research tool that quietly degrades is worse than one that stops. So a run that can’t read its data reports FAILED, not COMPLETE. The broker connection is probed with a hard timeout, so an expired token can never hang the pipeline. And the corporate-event calendar, once its dates go stale, returns UNKNOWN (unverified) rather than a confident all-clear it hasn’t earned.

fail-loud
coverage gate · bounded token probe · catalyst UNKNOWN ≠ NONE

04 · STATUS, STATED PLAINLY

The edge is not proven.

No profit is claimed and none exists yet. The instrument is early into accumulating the forward outcomes that will confirm or kill the backtest, and that clock is measured in months, not days.

What is finished is the instrument itself: honest, leakage-proof, and built to tell me the truth even when the truth is “we don’t know yet.” That discipline, not any claimed return, is the deliverable. 188 tests pass.
