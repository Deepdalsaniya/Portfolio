Case Study · Salesforce & Automation

Cutting a team's manual workload by 69% on the CRM they already owned.

The University of Maryland's Division of Student Affairs licensed Salesforce and Qualtrics but ran three core workflows by hand. I led a five-person team through requirements, design, build, and handover to automate them, returning roughly 18.5 hours of staff time every week.

01 · THE PROBLEM

Three manual workflows, one missing system of record.

DSA serves students, alumni, parents, and the wider University community across three of its offices, which UMD refers to as divisions: Career Services, the community/constituent function, and the Office of the Vice President. It ran on Salesforce but used almost none of its automation. Career-survey follow-up meant exporting Qualtrics responses and emailing personalized resources one student at a time. Community inquiries arrived across multiple shared inboxes with no case tracking, so responses were slow and there was no record of who handled what. Constituent data sat in scattered spreadsheets with missing contacts, making event outreach manual and error-prone.

Across these four task categories, staff spent a modeled 27 hours per week on mechanical work. The root cause was not effort. It was the absence of a single system of record and any automation on top of it.

03 · WHAT I BUILT

Three workstreams, each with a human in the loop.

i.

Survey automation

Qualtrics data ingested into Salesforce; record-triggered flows evaluate each response and auto-send matching Office of Career Services resources and scheduled follow-ups. Testing exposed duplicate emails to the same student, so I added an email-validation module that eliminated them.

ii.

Web-to-Case intake

Standardized forms replace the shared inboxes: each submission creates a tagged, routed case, auto-replies with a unique tracking number, and logs errors with a retry path. Staff now work from one queue with a full communication record.

iii.

Constituent CRM

Contact data moved out of spreadsheets into Salesforce, with standardized bulk import plus automated welcome and scheduled greeting emails for VP-office outreach, making event communication consistent and repeatable.

04 · HOW I RAN IT

Requirements to handover, traceable at every step.

1

Research & requirements

Mapped the client's real processes and pain points before designing anything.

2

Design

Data-flow diagrams, an entity-relationship model, a CRUD matrix, and a documented object/field schema, so every build traced to a requirement.

3

Build & test

Built the flows in a Salesforce sandbox and tested each workstream against client-approved 200-record datasets, iterating when tests surfaced defects.

4

Secure & review

Enforced role-based permissions and MFA on sensitive data; reviewed configuration with UMD's IT division to align with Salesforce architecture.

5

Handover

Delivered user guides and a training plan for DSA staff.

07 · LEARNINGS

The automation was the easy part.

  • Sandbox-only access meant email workflows could not run end to end against production, so we built temporary in-sandbox checks and validated delivery within licensing limits.
  • A shared Case object across departments limited how far we could customize page layouts for VP-office use, solved with tailored permissions and layouts rather than new objects.
  • Limited early access to the live environment created prototype-versus-production gaps, which we closed with a standing client review instead of assumptions.

The throughline: the flows were straightforward. The data model, the edge cases, and client alignment were where the real work lived.

08 · WHAT I'D DO DIFFERENTLY TODAY

From automated intake to intelligent intake.

I would replace the batch upload with a real-time API integration between Qualtrics and Salesforce, removing the largest source of latency and human error in the design.

More significantly, I would rebuild Web-to-Case as an AI-enabled intelligent intake workflow: an NLP layer that reads each inbound case, categorizes and prioritizes it, routes it to the right owner, and drafts a suggested response, with a human approval gate before anything sends. That turns a static form into a documented, versioned SOP the system executes and staff supervise, rather than tribal knowledge. The 2024 build proved that consolidating onto one platform and automating the mechanical steps returns real time to a team. The next version applies the same principle to the judgment-heavy steps, carefully, with a person still owning the decision.
